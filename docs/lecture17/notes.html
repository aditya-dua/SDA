Section 1: The Business Case

A ride platform must balance two competing outcomes: riders need cars quickly, while drivers need a reason to move toward high-demand zones. A multiplier can help rebalance the market—but a weak rule can create unfair prices, false scarcity, or reputational risk.

Demand sourceRide requests by zone. Answers: how many riders currently want a trip?
Supply sourceAvailable and occupied drivers. Answers: how much capacity can respond?
Context sourceRain, traffic, and event intensity. Answers: why might matching be difficult?

The 60-Second Scenario

Section 3: Pricing Logic and Guardrails

For each zone, the consumer counts requests received in the last 10 seconds, combines the latest driver supply and context, then calculates:

ratio = requests_in_last_10_seconds / max(available_drivers, 1) demand adjustment = max(ratio - 1, 0) × 0.35 weather adjustment = rain intensity × 0.20 traffic adjustment = max(traffic index - 0.50, 0) × 0.30 event adjustment = event intensity × 0.25 raw multiplier = 1 + all adjustments capped target = clamp(raw, 1.0, 2.5) final multiplier moves toward the target by at most ±0.20
GuardrailManagement Purpose
Minimum 1.0×This classroom model does not apply discounts.
Maximum 2.5×Limits extreme customer impact during shocks.
±0.20 per decisionAvoids abrupt jumps caused by one update.
Visible componentsEvery decision stores the uncapped proposal, capped/rate-limited result, factor contributions, and a human-readable reason.
Isolated databasesda_course_l17 cannot overwrite Lecture 10 or 16 data.

Decision question: Should rain increase price when demand remains below supply? The current formula says “slightly.” A team could instead use context only to explain demand pressure, not directly change price. There is no neutral choice—only a policy with consequences.

Panel A — Current Multiplier by Zone (Bar Gauge)

SELECT p.zone, p.final_multiplier AS value
FROM pricing_decisions p
JOIN (
  SELECT zone, MAX(decision_id) AS latest_id
  FROM pricing_decisions
  GROUP BY zone
) latest ON p.decision_id = latest.latest_id
WHERE p.zone REGEXP '${zone:regex}'
ORDER BY value DESC;

Panel B — Multiplier over Time (Time Series)

SELECT
  decision_time AS time,
  final_multiplier AS value,
  zone AS metric
FROM pricing_decisions
WHERE $__timeFilter(decision_time)
  AND zone REGEXP '${zone:regex}'
ORDER BY decision_time;

Panel C — Demand versus Supply (Time Series)

SELECT
  decision_time AS time,
  demand_window AS `Requests in 10s`,
  available_drivers AS `Available Drivers`
FROM pricing_decisions
WHERE $__timeFilter(decision_time)
  AND zone = '${zone}'
ORDER BY decision_time;

For this panel, select one zone rather than All.

Panel D — Latest Factor Breakdown (Table)

SELECT
  zone,
  demand_supply_ratio,
  demand_adjustment,
  weather_adjustment,
  traffic_adjustment,
  event_adjustment,
  final_multiplier,
  reason
FROM pricing_decisions
WHERE zone REGEXP '${zone:regex}'
ORDER BY decision_id DESC

SELECT
  decision_time,
  phase,
  zone,

  reason

Section 6: Interpret, Challenge, and Govern

What Students Should Observe

  1. Normal phase multipliers remain close to 1.0×.
  2. The CBD and Railway Station respond more strongly than other zones.
  3. The final multiplier moves gradually because each decision is rate-limited.
  4. Recovery lags the source change; smoothing trades responsiveness for stability.
  5. The audit table explains whether demand, weather, traffic, or an event contributed.

Policy Challenge — Change One Rule

Each group chooses one change, predicts the effect, edits surge_pricing_logic_17.py, reruns the scenario, and compares the dashboard:

  • Reduce the cap from 2.5× to 1.8×.
  • Remove weather as a direct price adjustment.
  • Increase the demand weight from 0.35 to 0.50.
  • Slow the maximum change from ±0.20 to ±0.10.
  • Require a minimum of 10 requests before any multiplier above 1.0×.

Fairness and Governance Discussion

RiskManagement QuestionPossible Control
Emergency price shockShould surge apply during floods or evacuations?Emergency-mode cap or disable rule
Geographic proxy biasDo some neighbourhoods face systematically higher prices?Zone fairness audit and outcome monitoring
False scarcityWhat if driver telemetry is delayed?Freshness checks; freeze multiplier on stale supply
Customer transparencyCan a rider understand why price increased?Reason codes and pre-booking disclosure
Driver gamingCan coordinated sign-offs create artificial shortage?Anomaly detection and minimum evidence window

60-Second Management Briefing

  1. Situation: Which zone changed, and when?
  2. Evidence: What happened to demand, supply, and context?
  3. Decision: Keep, cap, or suspend the multiplier?
  4. Guardrail: What customer or driver risk must be monitored?

Key takeaway: Dynamic pricing is not a formula alone. It is a governed decision system: multiple sources, stateful processing, explainable rules, operational monitoring, and explicit limits on harm.
